## OPENING (5 MINUTES)

**Talking Points:**
- Welcome everyone and thank them for attending

- Briefly mention your experience with HR systems
- Set expectations for the presentation

**Script:**
"Good morning/afternoon, everyone. Thank you all for taking time out of your busy schedules to be here today. My name is [Your Name], and I'm [Your Title]. I've been working with HR systems for [X years], and I'm excited to share something that I believe will significantly impact how we manage our post-selection hiring processes.

### Slide 2: The Challenge (2 minutes)

**Talking Points:**
- Start with a relatable problem
- Show data/statistics if available
- Make it personal (connect to audience pain points)
- Build urgency for solution

**Script:**
"Before I show you the solution, let me paint a picture of where we are today. I've been talking with many of you, and I've heard consistent challenges:

First, our candidate data is all over the place. We have spreadsheets, emails, different tools—it's hard to get a single view of where each candidate stands. How many of you have spent time hunting for candidate information across multiple systems? [Pause for hands]

Second, feedback collection is painful. We rely on email chains, and often feedback gets lost or delayed. We don't have a structured way to collect feedback from multiple interviewers.

Third, generating offer letters is time-consuming. Our HR team spends hours manually creating each offer, which is error-prone and delays the hiring process.

Fourth, we don't have a centralized place for policy management, salary definitions, or onboarding coordination. This leads to miscommunication and delays in the hiring process.

Finally, we lack visibility. Recruiters, HR managers, and interviewers can't easily track progress or collaborate effectively.

These challenges cost us time, money, and potentially good candidates who get frustrated with our slow process.

The good news? There's a better way."

## SYSTEM OVERVIEW (8 MINUTES)

### Slide 4: The Five User Roles (3 minutes)

**Talking Points:**
- Introduce each role clearly
- Explain their responsibilities
- Show how roles interact
- Emphasize security and access control

**Script:**
"The system is built around five distinct user roles, each with specific permissions and workflows. Let me walk you through each one:

**Super Admin** - This is the system administrator. They have full control of the system. They manage all users, configure system settings, and have access to all modules. If you're in IT or senior HR, this is your role. Super Admins can create new users, assign roles, reset passwords, and view system-wide analytics.

**HR Manager** - This role handles the post-selection HR processes. HR Managers generate offer letters, define salary structures, upload company policies, schedule inductions, and send communications to candidates. They're essentially the orchestrators of the hiring process after a candidate is selected. If you're in HR, this is likely your role.

**Recruiter** - Recruiters manage the candidate pipeline. They add candidates to the system, update their status as they progress through interview rounds, collect feedback from interviewers, and generate reports. They're the primary point of contact for candidate progression through the hiring funnel. If you're in recruiting, this is your role.

**Interviewer** - Interviewers have limited access. They can view candidate profiles and add feedback comments. This role is perfect for team members who participate in interviews but don't need full system access. They can see candidates and contribute feedback, but they can't modify candidate information or access sensitive data.

**Candidate** - This is an external role for job applicants. Candidates can view their profile, see offer letters, accept or decline offers, acknowledge company policies, and receive communications. They interact with the system through a dedicated candidate portal.

Each role has specific permissions and can only access the features relevant to their responsibilities. This ensures data security and maintains a clean, focused interface for each user.

For example, a Recruiter can't generate offers or define salaries. An Interviewer can't change candidate status. A Candidate can't see other candidates' information. This role-based approach protects sensitive data and prevents unauthorized actions."

**Pause for Effect:** 10 seconds

### Slide 5: Candidate Pipeline (3 minutes)

**Talking Points:**
- Show the complete pipeline
- Explain each stage
- Demonstrate filtering and search
- Show feedback integration

**Script:**
"Let's look at the Candidate Pipeline module, which is the heart of the system.

The pipeline tracks candidates through clearly defined stages. When a candidate applies, they start in the 'Applied' stage. As they progress through interviews, their status updates: Shortlisted, HR Round, Final Round, Feedback Submitted, Selected, or Rejected.

Recruiters can update a candidate's status with a single click. The system maintains a complete history of all status changes, so you can see exactly when and how a candidate progressed.

You can filter candidates by status to see, for example, all candidates currently in the Final Round. You can search by name or email to quickly find a specific candidate.

One of the most powerful features is that all feedback from different interviewers is visible in one place. You can see technical ratings, behavioral ratings, and detailed comments from each interviewer. This makes it easy to make final hiring decisions.

Everything updates in real-time. When a recruiter updates a candidate's status, everyone sees it immediately. There's no confusion about where a candidate stands."

**Pause for Effect:** 5 seconds

### Slide 6: Feedback System (2 minutes)

**Talking Points:**
- Explain structured feedback
- Show rating scales
- Demonstrate feedback history
- Emphasize collaboration

**Script:**
"The Feedback System is crucial for making good hiring decisions.

Interviewers rate candidates on two dimensions: Technical Skills and Behavioral Skills, each on a 1-5 scale. This gives you a quick snapshot of how the candidate performed.

Beyond ratings, interviewers can add detailed comments about their observations, strengths, and concerns. This qualitative feedback is often as important as the ratings.

For each feedback submission, the interviewer makes a clear Go or No-Go decision. This helps streamline the decision-making process.

The system maintains a complete history of all feedback. You can see who submitted feedback, when, and what they said. This creates accountability and helps with compliance.

Interviewers with limited access can add comments to existing feedback without changing the ratings. This allows collaboration without compromising data integrity.

Recruiters can see a summary of all feedback for a candidate, making it easy to make final hiring decisions. If you have three interviewers and they all say 'Go', that's a strong signal. If there's disagreement, you can see the details and make an informed decision."

**Pause for Effect:** 5 seconds

## KEY FEATURES (12 MINUTES)

### Slide 7: Offer Letters (3 minutes)

**Talking Points:**
- Show time savings
- Demonstrate customization
- Show sending process
- Track status

**Script:**
"Now let's look at Offer Letter generation, which is one of the most time-consuming processes in HR.

Currently, how long does it take to generate an offer letter? [Pause for response] Probably 1-2 hours, right? With this system, it takes about 5 minutes.

Here's how it works: HR Managers select a candidate from the 'Selected' candidates list. They fill in key details like the position, joining date, reporting manager, location, and hiring type. The system generates a professional offer letter with all the information.

The offer includes all the details the candidate needs to know: position, department, joining date, reporting manager, location, and any other relevant information.

Once generated, HR Managers can send the offer directly to the candidate via email. The candidate receives a notification and can access the offer in their portal.

The system tracks the status of each offer. Is it pending (waiting for response)? Has the candidate accepted? Or declined? You can see at a glance.

Candidates can download and print their offer letters for their records.

The system maintains a complete history of all offers, including versions and changes. This is great for compliance and documentation."

**Pause for Effect:** 5 seconds

### Slide 8: Salary Structure (3 minutes)

**Talking Points:**
- Show comprehensive components
- Demonstrate calculations
- Show time savings
- Emphasize accuracy

**Script:**
"The Salary Structure module automates one of the most complex HR tasks—salary definition and calculation.

You define all earnings components: Basic Salary, House Rent Allowance, Dearness Allowance, and Special Allowance. You also define all deductions: Provident Fund, Employee State Insurance, Tax Deducted at Source, and Professional Tax.

Here's where the magic happens: The system automatically calculates:
- Gross Salary (total earnings)
- Total Deductions (sum of all deductions)
- Net Salary (what the candidate actually receives)
- Annual CTC (gross salary × 12 months)

As you enter values, calculations update in real-time. You can see exactly what the candidate will receive.

Let me show you an example. If you enter:
- Basic: 50,000
- HRA: 10,000
- DA: 5,000
- Special Allowance: 5,000
- Total Earnings: 70,000

And deductions:
- PF: 5,000
- ESI: 1,000
- TDS: 3,000
- Professional Tax: 500
- Total Deductions: 9,500

The system calculates:
- Gross: 70,000
- Net: 60,500
- Annual CTC: 840,000

All automatically. No manual calculations. No errors.

You can create templates for different roles or departments, then reuse them for similar positions. This saves time and ensures consistency.

The system maintains a complete record of all salary structures, so you can track changes and maintain audit trails."

**Pause for Effect:** 5 seconds

**Talking Points:**
- Show policy upload process
- Demonstrate acknowledgment tracking
- Emphasize compliance

**Script:**
"The Policy Management module ensures all candidates acknowledge company policies before joining.

Candidates can view all company policies in their portal. They can download and read each policy.

HR Managers can see which candidates have acknowledged which policies. This ensures everyone has acknowledged all required policies before joining.

You can generate reports showing acknowledgment status for all candidates. This is useful for compliance documentation and ensures you have proof that candidates acknowledged policies."

**Talking Points:**

## PRESENTATION DELIVERY TIPS

### Pacing
- Speak at a moderate pace (not too fast, not too slow)
- Pause after key points to let them sink in
- Vary your pace to maintain interest
- Slow down for important information

### Tone
- Use an enthusiastic but professional tone
- Show genuine excitement about the product
- Be confident in what you're presenting
- Relate to audience concerns

### Body Language
- Stand near the presentation area
- Make eye contact with different audience members
- Use gestures to emphasize points
- Avoid pacing back and forth
- Keep your hands visible (not in pockets)

### Engagement
- Ask rhetorical questions
- Use real-world examples
- Show before/after comparisons
- Encourage questions
- Listen actively to responses

### Handling Objections
- Listen to the full objection
- Acknowledge the concern
- Provide a thoughtful response
- Relate back to benefits
- Offer to discuss further if needed
